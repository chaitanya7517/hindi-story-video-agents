# Voice research: faster, still-legal Hindi voices (27 Nov 2026)

Written by the Voice Research agent. Goal: stop spending about 8 hours of CPU time on the voices for each video, and keep every licence safe for a **monetized** channel.

**Our setup now:** IndicF5 (MIT licence) on a 4-core CPU with no GPU. It takes about 3-5 minutes per line, so about 100 lines take about 8 hours.
**Characters we need:** Dadi (grandmother), a girl and a boy aged 6-9, adult men (king, servant, a deep calm god voice), an adult woman, and a squeaky comic mouse. Hindi only, written in Devanagari.

**How this was done:** I used web search and read the Hugging Face model cards, the vendor pricing pages and the platform rules. I also read our `tools/voice_f5.py`, `tools/voice_worker.py` and the IndicF5 `model.py` that is already downloaded. I did not install or download any model and did not use any API key.
**Two warnings:**
- Nothing here was listened to or timed on our machine. Speed numbers marked "est." are my estimates.
- Prices change often. Rupee figures use **US$1 = ₹88** and leave out GST unless the line says otherwise.

## 1. Short answer

| Goal | What to do | Time or cost per 15-min video |
|---|---|---|
| **Best quality, free** | Keep IndicF5 and our cloned voices, but run it on a **Kaggle free GPU** (T4) with the official `kaggle kernels push` / `kernels output` commands. | est. 15-40 min of GPU instead of 8 h of CPU. ₹0 |
| **Fastest free (no GPU)** | Tune IndicF5 on the CPU: `nfe_step=16`, 4 threads in one worker, shorter reference clips. | est. 2-4 h instead of 8 h. ₹0 |
| **Cheapest paid** | **Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS** (style prompts such as "old grandmother, warm, slow"). Backup: **Sarvam Bulbul v4 Flash** (Indian voices). | Gemini about ₹18 (about ₹37 from 1 Jan 2027). Sarvam about ₹31 with GST |
| **₹0 paid API (needs a card on file)** | Google Cloud **Chirp 3 HD** Hindi voices, inside the free 1 million characters a month. | ₹0 for up to about 75 videos a month. Adult voices only, no cloning |

**My recommendation:** move IndicF5 to Kaggle first. It keeps the voices we already have, so quality stays the same and every video sounds consistent. It costs nothing, and it takes the voice step from a whole night to well under an hour. Do the CPU tuning too, as the fallback for weeks when the Kaggle quota runs out. Try Gemini Flash TTS on one video as a paid comparison, because it can act emotions from a text prompt, which IndicF5 cannot do.

**Child voices:** no free model ships a real Hindi child voice. The only good route is **cloning**, using IndicF5, Chatterbox, Maya2 or Audio8 with a child-like reference clip that we have rights to: one we generated ourselves, or a recording made with a parent's consent. That is what we already do. An adult voice pitched up sounds fake. For the mouse, cloning plus a +3 to +5 semitone pitch shift in post-production is fine, because the mouse is meant to sound cartoonish.

## 3. Free GPU, legitimately

| Platform | What you get (free) | Automation | Rules to respect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Kaggle Notebooks**, best fit | About **30 GPU hours a week** (it "floats" a little week to week). T4 x2 or P100, 16 GB each. Up to 12 h per session. Needs a **phone-verified** account for GPU and internet. | **Yes, officially**: the Kaggle CLI does `kaggle kernels push -p folder --accelerator NvidiaTeslaT4` (it runs the notebook in the background), then `kaggle kernels status` and `kaggle kernels output -p ./out`. | One person per account (no sharing). Do not make extra accounts to farm quota. Accounts are tied to a phone number. No crypto mining. Keep the notebook **private** (our voice references are in it). The IndicF5 weights are gated, so the notebook needs a Hugging Face token stored as a Kaggle **Secret** (the user creates it, never in code). |
| **Google Colab free** | T4 when available. Up to 12 h, with idle timeouts. Limits are not published and change. | Not really. Free runtimes that bypass the notebook UI or use remote control (SSH and similar) get terminated. Built for clicking "Run all" by hand. | No multiple accounts to get around limits. No deepfakes (only clone voices we have rights to). |
| **Hugging Face ZeroGPU** | **5 min a day** on a free account (PRO: 40 min a day for $9 a month). Fast GPU (half an RTX Pro 6000). A free account can host 2 ZeroGPU Spaces if the account is 30+ days old. | `gradio_client` can call a Space and uses your quota | 5 min a day covers only about a third of one video. Too small, except for retakes. Hammering other people's public Spaces (such as the IndicF5 demo) for batch jobs is poor manners and they get rate-limited. |
| **Lightning AI** | Free credits that keep being **cut**: reports range from 15 credits a month (about 22 T4 h) to a Nov 2026 cut to about 50 "starter" hours. Lightning says it may change this at any time. | Yes (Studios, CLI) | Treat it as a backup. Check lightning.ai/pricing first. |

**Kaggle maths (est.):** 15 min of audio at a T4 RTF of about 0.3-0.5 is about 5-8 min of pure generation. Add model loading, retries and the ref-audio overhead, and one video takes about **15-40 GPU minutes**. 30 h a week then covers dozens of videos, far more than we need.

**Kaggle plan:**
1. Upload `voices/*.wav` and the line list as a private Kaggle dataset, using `kaggle datasets version`.
2. Write a notebook that installs IndicF5's code, loads the model on CUDA, loops over the lines (the same logic as `voice_worker.py`) and writes the WAVs to `/kaggle/working`.
3. Run `kernels push`, poll `kernels status`, then `kernels output`.

## 5. Making IndicF5 faster on our CPU

The code already supports these settings. `IndicF5/model.py` calls `infer_process(...)` with no `nfe_step`, so it gets the default **32 steps, cfg 2.0**. To change that, `voice_f5.py` can call `f5_tts.infer.utils_infer.infer_process` itself with the model's `ema_model` and `vocoder`.

| Trick | Expected speed-up | Expected quality loss |
|---|---|---|
| **`nfe_step=16`** (instead of 32) | about **2x** (time scales with steps) | **Small.** On the Fast F5-TTS paper's tests, 16 steps scored about the same as 32 (word error rate 2.29 vs 2.37, MOS 3.97 vs 3.93; English tests on GPU). Try 16 first. |
| `nfe_step=8-12`, or the "EPSS" 7-step schedule from the Fast F5-TTS paper | 3-4x | Small to noticeable. More breathiness and flatter emotion. EPSS needs a code change. Listen before using it. |
| **Use all 4 cores in one worker**: `torch.set_num_threads(4)` (the default in `voice_worker.py` is 2) | 1.3-1.8x if only one worker is running. About the same total if two 2-thread workers already run together. | None |
| **Shorter reference clips** (our clips are 5.5-9.9 s, for example `shyamlal.wav` is 9.9 s) | The model processes the reference plus the new line together, so a 5-6 s reference on a short line saves up to about 30-40% | Slight loss of voice likeness if the clip is too short (keep at least 5 s, and clean audio) |
| **Merge short lines by the same speaker** into one call, then split on silence | Saves the per-call overhead | Risky: splitting can cut words. Medium effort. |
| `cfg_strength=0` (turns off the second guidance pass, which halves the work) | about 2x | **Big.** Worse pronunciation and voice likeness. Not recommended. |
| int8 dynamic quantization (`torch.ao.quantization.quantize_dynamic` on the Linear layers) or ONNX (community `IndicF5-ONNX`, `DakeQQ/F5-TTS-ONNX`) | Maybe 1.3-1.7x (seen on other TTS models, **not** measured for F5) | Unknown, possibly small. The IndicF5-ONNX card says it is "not realtime on Apple M1 CPU". Effort is high for a small gain. |

**Realistic CPU target (est.):** 16 steps + 4 threads + references of 6 s or less gets about 1-1.5 min per line, so **about 2-3 h per video** instead of 8 h. Check it with a 5-line A/B test, listening to the 32-step and 16-step versions side by side.

## 6. Honest downsides

- **Kaggle** quota and rules can change, and GPUs are sometimes queued. We depend on one account and must never create a second. Gated IndicF5 weights mean the user has to create a Hugging Face token and store it as a Kaggle secret.
- **IndicF5** still skips words in long lines and mis-reads numbers. Keep lines short, write numbers as words, and keep the `sane_length` retry. It cannot be told to act an emotion: the emotion comes from the reference clip, so a character may need 2 reference clips (calm and excited).
- **Newer models** (Maya2, Audio8, Chatterbox V3 Hindi, Svara, Indic-Speak) have **no independent Hindi listening tests**. The quality claims are the vendors' own. Maya2 and Indic-Speak need a big GPU. Indic-Speak's licence has conditions.
- **Chatterbox** puts a watermark in every file. That is fine for us, but it is there.
- **APIs** have no consistent cloning (except ElevenLabs), so Dadi may sound slightly different from one video to the next. Gemini's free tier uses our scripts for training, and its prices **double on 1 Jan 2027**. Every API needs an account, a key and a card (Google and Azure free tiers included).
- **Child voices** are a weak spot everywhere. Cloning from a self-made child-like reference is the only good option. Only clone voices we have rights to (all the model cards say so, and so do Colab's rules against deepfakes).
- Speed estimates are not measured on our machine, and prices were read in November 2026.
